# Scene 4 — The Cage Cannot Be Opened (NSC / Sarcophagus)

---

## 1. Voice Script

### 🎭 Style Instructions (System Prompt for TTS)

Voice: Charon
Style: Documentary narrator, now more analytical. The focus shifts from horror to engineering reality.
Pacing: Steady, explanatory. Help the viewer understand why a billion-dollar structure can't just be opened.
Tone: There's a bitter irony here — we built a cage to protect us from the core, but the cage also makes removal impossible.
Emphasis: "Contain, not remove", "€1.5 billion", "February 2025", "dismantle it"

### 📝 The Annotated Script (User Prompt for TTS)

[Analytical, steady.]
In the immediate aftermath, the Soviets built a sarcophagus — hastily poured concrete and steel beams — to seal the destroyed reactor from the environment.
[pause 1s]
It was never meant to last. By the early 2000s, it was crumbling.
[pause 1.5s]

[Explaining]
So the international community built something new. The New Safe Confinement — a giant steel arch, 110 meters tall, 260 meters wide — built on rails so it could be slid over the old sarcophagus.
[pause 1.5s]
Cost: one point five billion euros.
[pause 1.5s]

[Emphasis]
But here's the critical detail: the arch was designed to contain. Not to allow removal.
[pause 1.5s]
It keeps the dust in. It keeps the rain out. It buys time.
[pause 1s]
But it was never designed for humans or robots to dismantle the core from inside.
[pause 2s]

[Darker]
And then, in February 2025, a drone carrying a high-explosive warhead struck the arch. It punched a hole in the confinement. The airtight seal was lost.
[pause 1.5s]
Repairs are expected to cost five hundred million euros — and the structure is only certified to operate until 2029.
[pause 2s]

[Slow, closing]
Even if we wanted to remove the core... we would have to dismantle the cage that protects us from it.
[pause 1.5s]
The moment we open the sarcophagus, we release radioactive dust into the atmosphere. We would create a second Chernobyl — by trying to fix the first one.
[pause 2s]
